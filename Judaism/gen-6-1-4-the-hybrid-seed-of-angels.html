New Testament Writings

There are good reasons to believe that both Peter and Jude are referring to the sons of God of Genesis 6:1-4. First, the examples they give of God’s destruction of the wicked can all be found in the Hebrew Bible. I.e., the stories of Noah and the flood, Sodom and Gomorrah, and Israel’s exodus and punishment in the desert are all found in the Pentateuch. This means that the readers are expected to be able to locate the fall of the angels there as well, and Gen. 6:1-4 is the only place where they would be able to do so.

Second, Jude states that the gross sexual immorality of Sodom and Gomorrah in going after strange flesh, i.e., unnatural relations, was similar to the sin of the angels. This means that the angels were also guilty of committing gross sexual perversion. Now what could be more perverted than angelic beings having sex with human women? After all, it doesn’t get any more unnatural than that!

Third, Jude quotes Enoch 1:9 in vv. 14-15, the very book where the sons of God are explicitly identified as angels. Therefore, Jude’s readers could not help but make the connection between the fallen angels of v. 6 and the heavenly beings of Gen. 6:1-4.
