Gregory Nyssa, the Wonderworker & the Trinity

September 28, 2026
trinity church-history theology #Beautified

The Cappadocian father Gregory of Nyssa wrote On the Life of Gregory Thaumaturgus (Life of Gregory Thaumaturgus) (bishop and missionary in Pontus, S00687), which is “a biographical panegyric praising the 3rd-century bishop and miracle worker [1, 2, 3].” Nyssa had “delivered [this] as a homily on the saint’s feast, recounting the manifold miracles he performed during his lifetime. Written in Greek in Asia Minor, in the late 370s or the 380s.”

Gregory of Nyssa's paternal grandmother, Macrina the Elder, was a direct disciple of Gregory Thaumaturgus. This means that the Wonderworker would have had a very close relationship with Nyssa’s family, explaining why Nyssa would have spoken and written of him. As such, Nyssa becomes a very important witness to the Wonderworker being a full-blown orthodox Trinitarian, since the former would not have written positively of the latter if he wasn’t. This is because Nyssa himself was a full-blown Trinitarian who was one of the greatest defenders of Trinitarian monotheism.

I cite his epistle to Ablabius to illustrate this fact. All emphasis will be mine.

Nyssa’s letter displays a masterful articulation and defense of the essential unity of the most Holy and Glorious Trinity, and why the three Divine Persons are only one God and not three.

Therefore, since Nyssa wrote glaringly of the Wonderworker, and since his paternal grandmother was a devout student of the Wonderworker, this proves that Thaumaturgus was indeed an orthodox Trinitarians who loved, worshiped and glorified the same Holy Trinity that Nyssa did. In fact, it is obvious that Nyssa’s Trinitarianism flows from what his grandmother would have learned from the Wonderworker and passed on to her family.
