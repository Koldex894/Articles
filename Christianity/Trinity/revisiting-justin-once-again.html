Revisiting Justin Once Again!

September 3, 2026

The distortion of Justin Martyr’s writings by anti-Trinitarians seems to be never-ending. Instead of allowing this blessed and holy martyr of Christ to articulate his Trinitarian beliefs in his own way, these individuals insist on reading their own views into his statements in order to make him a witness against the glorious Trinity. An example of this is to interpret what Justin wrote in his first apology concerning God the Father being more kingly and just than Christ as proof of his subordinating the Son to a lesser divine position.

In light of this distortion, I have decided to once again set the record straight by examining this blessed martyr’s words in the immediate and over all contexts of his works in order to show that this wasn’t his intended meaning. I will prove that Justin’s language points to the Father being the Source of the Son’s rule, power, justice, divinity, etc., precisely because he is the inoriginate Begetter of the uncreated Word whom he begot from himself, out of his own nature from before all creation. I will also quote places where Justin states that Christians worship the Triad, meaning, the Father in the first place, the Son second, and the prophetic/divine Spirit third. From there I will cite Justin affirming that Jesus is the Lord God YHWH whom the patriarchs and prophets saw, since this will prove that Justin did believe that Jesus is the Almighty King who is just as kingly and righteous as his Father. This is because the Bible is clear that YHWH is perfectly and incomparably righteous and holy, and that there is none who is higher or greater than him in status.

Note how Justin believes that Jesus is the One who appeared to Abraham and destroyed Sodom, and who caused Sarah to conceive. Jesus is also the Angel who appeared to Jacob and was the Man whom the patriarch wrestled. Christ is even said to be the Angel in the burning bush and the Captain that appeared to Joshua.

Most interesting is Justin’s analogy of a man begetting a word without it being cut off from himself, and likening it to the Son’s begetting from before all creatures. This shows that Justin believed that the Son remained inseparable from the Father, since the begetting did not result in God being cut off from his rational Power/Word.
