Jesus As God In Luke-Acts

The fourth, line of evidence which proves that the disciples worshiping Jesus as God has to do with Christ’s promise that he will send to them the Holy Spirit from the Father, a promise which Luke says was fulfilled on the day of Pentecost:

What makes this event interesting–in fact shocking–is that Peter spoke to the Jews who were witnessing this miracle and told them that what they were hearing and seeing was the fulfillment of the prophecy in Joel 2:28-32 where God said that a day would come when he himself–not someone else–would pour out his Spirit upon all his servants:

And yet according to both Jesus and the blessed Apostle, it was actually Christ who sent forth and poured out the Holy Spirit upon Jesus’ followers!

This again demonstrates that Jesus is Yahweh Incarnate since he performs the very functions which the Hebrew Bible ascribes to Yahweh alone, namely, pouring out or sending forth God’s Spirit to indwell and empower the people of God to love and obey the Lord:

Even the Quran acknowledges that it is God or Allah who sends forth the Spirit to strengthen his followers:

Fifth, Jesus informed his followers that the prophets themselves testified that forgiveness of sins shall be proclaimed in Jesus’ name to all the nations, meaning that salvation comes from Christ, by his authority, to all who believe in him. This explains why all throughout Luke-Acts we find Jesus’ followers performing miracles in the name of Christ, and declaring that salvation comes only through faith in Jesus’ name:

However, the OT teaches that Yahweh grants salvation and forgiveness of sins for the sake of his own name: 

This again affirms that Jesus’ followers viewed him in the same way that the OT saints viewed Yahweh, which explains why they invoked his name or authority in the same way that the OT believers invoked the name of Yahweh God!

In light of the foregoing, it is abundantly clear that according to Luke 24:52 the disciples were worshiping Jesus as God Incarnate. Contrary to the assertion of Islam, they were not simply honoring him as a man whom God had exalted.
