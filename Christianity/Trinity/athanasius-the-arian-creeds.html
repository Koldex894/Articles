Athanasius & the Arian Creeds

June 19, 2025
church-history theology trinity jesus-christ christianity orthodoxy #Beautified 2025

This lengthy extract is from Athanasius’ De Synodis.

It chronicles the beliefs of Arius and those who believed like him, and further details some of the many councils and creeds which followed after Nicaea in order to either overturn it or revise it.

What makes these creedal formulations rather peculiar, in fact outright bizarre, is that Arius and his ilk taught that the Son was created or brought into being before the creation of time and the ages, and that time and the ages were actually created by/through the Son, which makes absolutely no sense.

After all, if the Son created all time and all the ages then he must be by his very nature timeless, ageless and therefore uncreated. As such, there could never be a moment when the Son did not exist as God and as one with the Father.

There were other groups who actually affirmed that the Son has always existed, and that there was never a time when he did not exist. These folks further taught that it was the Son to whom the Father spoke the words of Genesis 1:26, “Let Us make man in Our image and after Our likeness…”

They further stressed that the Son is the God that appeared throughout the Old Testament to the patriarchs and prophets, being the One who gave the Law to Moses and that very YHWH whom Genesis 19:24 states was on earth summoning brimstone and sulfur from the YHWH who was in the heavens above. These so-called Christians even went as far as to anathematize or condemn any person who would deny that these biblical citations refer to the Son appearing as God and the One with whom the Father conversed.

What makes this particular group all the more interesting, in fact outright baffling, is that they even employed the terms Triad and Trinity describe the fact that there are three eternal subsistences or Persons in the Godhead!

Another interesting aspect of the theology of these individuals is that they refused to use such terms as “essence”, “nature’, etc., on the grounds that the Scriptures do not employ such terminology to describe the Son’s relationship to the Father. In this sense, they were a kind of Sola Scriptura Protestants anachronistically speaking, in fact a very extreme kind who just wanted to employ scriptural language to discuss the nature of the Godhead.

They realized just how difficult this was since the inspired Scriptures were not written to address the kind of issues and challenges that arose centuries later by folks who were employing highly technical and sophisticated philosophical terminology and jargon to express their views and challenges.
