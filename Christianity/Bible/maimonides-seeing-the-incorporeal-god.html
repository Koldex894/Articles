Moses Maimonides was a 12th–13th century AD Jewish theologian/philosopher who was heavily influenced by the Greek notions of God’s incorporeality and impassibility, which caused him to allegorize and anthropomorphize much of the Hebrew Bible.

Maimonides explained away OT verses, which speak of God appearing invisible, tangible ways, often as a Man. One of the ways he did so was to argue that these appearances were not of God himself but of something other, such as God’s Glory, Word or Voice.
