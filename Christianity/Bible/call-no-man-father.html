In this post I will show how this interpretation pits Scripture against Scripture, creating contradictions within the canon when in fact there are none. I will then add a corrective by explain what our Lord meant.

To begin with, we find all throughout the Hebrew Bible where prophets, kings, and administrators are called “father” or where others are said to be their children, obviously in a spiritual or metaphorical sense:

Now this means that we either have a contradiction in Scripture, as well as a clear act of disobedience on the part of Jesus’ followers who went around calling themselves fathers to believers, and addressing their disciples as their own spiritual children. Or this is simply another case of Protestants misinterpreting the blessed words of our Lord.

A closer look at the context helps us appreciate the meaning of our Lord:

As the readers can see, our Lord was refuting blind allegiance to religious authorities, submitting to them with wholehearted and unquestioning allegiance, since even religious figures can be deceivers and themselves be deceived.

Therefore, the point isn’t that one cannot use these titles for human figures and authorities. Rather, the issue is one of attitude, namely, believers in Christ are not to look to any fallible human leader with absolute trust and undivided loyalty, since such allegiance and devotion are to be given to the Godhead alone. Believers are to give total allegiance and must perfectly, completely submit to the Father, Son and Spirit, since they can never be wrong, and can and will never mislead or deceive anyone. Since the Father, Son and Spirit are truth itself, they can be fully trusted and believed in to guide believers to all truth, having complete trust that the Godhead knows what’s best for believers since they desire to guide everyone unto the truth of salvation.

This is the kind of trust and allegiance that can never be given to any human authority on earth, since mankind by nature is imperfect, fallen, sinful, capricious, selfish, and duplicitous. As the scriptures themselves proclaim:

This explains why the holy Apostles, though they were the leaders and foundation of the Church,

Did not exploit their status and position by taking advantage of and/or lording it over the flock. Instead, they used their authority to serve believers in order to ensure that no one would idolize them or make them much greater than they actually were:

In adopting this attitude, the blessed Apostles were faithfully carrying out the directive of our glorious Lord Jesus who epitomized and exemplified humble servitude:
