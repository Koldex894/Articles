OT References to Hell in the King James Bible

September 17, 2016
bible bible-study theology christianity eternal-life #Beautified 2016

The Authorized King James Version (AV) of the Holy Bible renders two different words as hell, namely the Hebrew term Sheol (Greek – Hades) and the Greek phrase Gehenna.

Sheol/Hades typically refers to the abode of the disembodied dead, the place where the spirits/souls of the departed go to when they physically die. In certain instances, it simply means the common grave.

And up until Jesus’ physical resurrection and ascension into heaven everyone went to this spiritual place/dimension, with the righteous being in a state of peace and bliss in contrast to the wicked who undergo agony and torment as they remain there until the last day (cf. Genesis 15:15; 25:8-9; 1 Samuel 28:11-19; Luke 16:19-23).

Now, however, only the spirits/souls of the wicked enter there, whereas the spirits/souls of the righteous go to be with the risen Lord in heaven (cf. 2 Corinthian 5:1-10; Philippians 1:21, 23; Hebrews 12:22-24; Revelation 6:9-11).

Gehenna, on the other hand, is the place where both the body and spirit/soul of a person is sent to experience unending punishment after the general resurrection and judgment day (cf. Matthew 10:28; Mark 9:43-49; Revelation 14:9-11; 19:20; 20:10-15).
