Gelasian Decree Pope Damasus & the Canon

June 6, 2025
canon church-history catholicism apocrypha bible #Beautified 2025

The Gelasian Decree (Decretum Gelasianum) is a Latin document dating from the 6th century AD, and is believed to be based on the decree of Pope Gelasius I (492-496 AD). 

The document mentions the canon list ratified by the Council (Synod) of Rome under the authority of Pope Damasus in 382 AD, a list which contains the deuterocanonicals. It further highlights the primacy of Peter and that of the Church of Rome, which the document states is first among the major sees in terms of status and authority. This would naturally extend to the authority of the Popes/Bishops of Rome.

What makes this decree all the more fascinating is that it even lists a number of books, which were forbidden to be read by the churches. Some of these writings include those of Tertullian, the Shepherd of Hermas, the Acts of Paul and Thecla, the Gospel of James, the infancy of the Savior and that of the nativity of the Savior and of Mary. It further exhorts the faithful to receive (accept) the writings of the holy men of the Church such as St. Augustine and St. Jerome.

The document also states that certain writings of Origen, specifically those approved by St. Jerome, can also be read in the Churches, as well as Eusebius’ Church History, though with some caution.

I quote the document in its entirety for the benefit of the readers. All emphasis will be mine.

Decretum Gelasianum (English translation).
