Micah: The Trinitarian Prophet

The prophet Micah utters an amazing proclamation, which seems to point to a Divine Plurality:

Micah speaking in the place of Israel states that YHWH will plead on their behalf and will justify or make things right for them. What makes this rather perplexing is the fact that YHWH is depicted as making a defense for Israel in order to acquit them. The question this raises is, plead to or with whom, and why does he need to do so?

This same YHWH is further depicted as Shepherding his people with his scepter, i.e., by virtue of being their King who removes their sins completely:

Yet earlier in his inspired writing, the prophet announced the coming of a human Ruler out of Bethlehem who would personally Shepherd his fellow Israelites by the authority of YHWH his God:

What makes this King so amazing is that he is not just a Man, but is an eternal Being who has been active from the very beginning of creation. In fact, Israel’s Ruler is depicted as being as old as YHWH himself since it is YHWH who is said to be from the beginning, from the days of eternity:

Both Jewish and Christian sources identify Micah’s Ruler as the King Messiah, who is Jesus according to the NT writings:

It now begins to make sense. The YHWH who pleads and intercedes on behalf of his people in order to vindicate them is not the Father. Rather, he is the Messianic King who is personally distinct from God, and yet truly divine in nature.

In other words, it is this Divine Messiah that intercedes before his God and Father for all those who put their trust in him, since he comes to remove all their sins.

This is precisely what the NT teaches in respect to Jesus.

Being the unique, divine Son of God that became Man, Jesus is both personally distinct from God, while also being identical to God in nature. As such, Jesus atones for the sins of the world and makes intercession for all those who give themselves to him:

Jesus is also the uncreated Word by whom all created things came into existence and who is the true Light of creation that came into the world in order to Shepherd and save his flock:

We thus have a clear indication within Micah of there being two Divine Persons, one of whom becomes a human being in order to Shepherd Israel as their King.

The foregoing helps us better appreciate why the prophet quotes YHWH as referring to another who is also YHWH:

Rather than YHWH speaking in the first person he instead refers to YHWH in the third person when speaking of his coming to rule in Mount Zion. In light of the above, this is to be explained as YHWH the Father speaking of YHWH the Son coming to Zion in order to be born of a woman so he can begin reigning over Israel as a fellow Israelite.
