Polycarp affirms the physical death, resurrection and ascension of Jesus into heaven to be seated on a throne alongside of God. The blessed martyr also attests that every created thing that breathes is to give Christ latreuo, which is the worship that God alone is to receive:

Polycarp even praises Paul by calling him glorious.

What makes this all the more interesting is how the beloved Bishop clearly refutes sola fide, since he interprets Eph. 2:8-9 to mean that God grants salvation as a gracious gift to the believers who are doing his will and obeying his commandments.

Polycarp proclaims that Jesus Christ came into the flesh, died for our sins, was raised from the dead, being the Lord that forgives sins and will comes to judge. He clearly affirms that the dead shall be resurrected, and expressly says that anyone who denies the coming resurrection and judgment of mankind is Satan’s firstborn. The holy martyr also states that believers are the slaves of Christ, and that the elders and deacons belong to both God and Jesus.

Another Epilogue, From the Moscow Manuscript

This letter is remarkable in that it shows the holy Bishop’s fearlessness and willingness to be burned alive and killed for the glory of Christ. It is also significant since it confirms that St. Irenaeus was a disciple of the beloved St. Polycarp, and further shows how the early Christians were Trinitarians who worshiped and glorified the Son and the Holy Spirit along with the Father.
