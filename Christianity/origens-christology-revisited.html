Origen’s Christology Revisited

April 24, 2025
church-history theology jesus-christ trinity christianity #Beautified 2025

The second-third century AD Christian writer, theologian and martyr Origen was truly an astounded figure in Church history. His insights, though flawed in so many ways, were at the same time remarkable, which is perhaps why his writings were preserved even though he was later condemned as a heretic.

In this post I will be highlighting some of what this brilliant theologian wrote in respect to the Person, work and titles of our Lord Jesus Christ, in order to show just how amazing some of his claims were.

For instance, Origen describes Christ as the uncreated and timelessly generated Wisdom of God, being God the Word, the Creator of all things through and from whom all life came. He also identified Christ as the First and the Last, the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, as well as the Almighty of Rev. 1:8, 17-18; 22:13. Origen even stated that Christ is the Angel of God mentioned in OT texts such as the Greek version of Isaiah 9:6. Origen was of the opinion that the Church is spiritual Israel and that texts such as Isaiah 42 and 49 called Christ Jacob and Israel.

Some other aspects of Origen’s teachings include his belief that the beginning (arche) mentioned in Jn. 1:1-2 is a title for Wisdom, and based this on Prov. 8:22 where Wisdom is said to be the beginning of God’s works. He then reasoned from this fact that when the Word is said to be in the beginning with God that this literally meant that the Word was inherent in Wisdom itself.

Origen further taught that Christ died to atone for and save all rational souls, not just human beings, and that those human souls who had never heard of Christ were not accountable for their sins since they didn’t know any better.

One remarkable aspect of Origen’s exegesis is how interprets the words of our Lord to the rich young ruler, when the latter called him “Good Master.” Origen takes Christ’s statement, “Why do you call me Good? There is none good but God alone,” to mean that the Lord was affirming that his essential goodness is the exact same goodness that originates from the Father. Origen used this saying to prove that the Son shares in the very same goodness of the Father since the Son’s nature is that of the Father’s, having been begotten of the Father.

For Origen, our Lord’s words meant that he fully shared in and possessed the exact identical divine essence belonging to his Father, which is precisely why the Son’s goodness could not be other than the very goodness inherent in the Father himself.
