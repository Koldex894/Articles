Therefore, Jesus has to actually be God in the flesh in order for him to be a co-occupant of God’s throne in heaven.

Answering A Potential Objection

Now someone may object at this point and argue that the Holy Bible mentions other thrones that are occupied by different individuals, such as the twelve apostles of Christ and the twenty-four elders in heaven mentioned in Revelation (cf. Matthew 19:28; Luke 22:29-30; Revelation 4:4; 20:4), and yet no Christian assumes that they too are God.

There are several problems with this argument. First, Jesus isn’t simply sitting on a throne, but on God’s own throne from which he rules over all creation, which leads us to the second point. All of these other individuals are forever subject to Christ since he reigns over them as King of kings and Lord of lords (cf. Revelation 1:5; 17:14; 19:16). Third, these other rulers are required to show their subjection to Christ by worshiping him in the same way that they worship God the Father, since they are called to serve both God and Christ forever:

In fact, contrast the way the twenty-four elders and four living creatures worship God,

With the worship given to the Lord Jesus:

Every created thing in the entire creation is depicted as worshiping Jesus in the same exact way that God the Father is worshiped, thereby proving that Jesus isn’t a mere creature but God Almighty in the flesh. Otherwise, God would not allow Jesus to receive such worship since that would be both blasphemous and idolatrous. Nor is Revelation the only inspired writing which states that every creature must worship Christ as they do the Father:

Remarkably, Paul adopts the following OT text which exhorts all the nations to turn and worship Yahweh since he alone is God,

And applies it to the worship which God requires every creature to give to Christ himself as their risen Lord!

Now this blessed Apostle could only do this if he was convinced and truly believed that Jesus Christ is Yahweh God in the flesh (even though he isn’t the Father or the Holy Spirit).

Thus, the worship which Jesus receives simply further confirms the fact that Christ is ruling from heaven as God Incarnate, and not as a mere created agent who represents God.
