Tertullian: Jesus as Uncreated Wisdom & Creatio Ex Nihilo

September 30, 2026
jesus god wisdom theology church-history #Beautified

In this post I will be citing from Tertullian’s response Against Hermogenes, who taught that matter was uncreated which, by extension, implies that evil always existed since certain Greek philosophers deemed matter to be evil by its very nature. Tertullian brilliantly refutes the erroneous assertion that matter is eternal and then sets forth to prove that God’s Wisdom and Word (who then became the Man Jesus) brought all creation into being from nothing, i.e., creatio ex nihilo. In doing so, Tertullian emphasizes the point of God’s Wisdom/Word being intrinsic to God’s very own uncreated Being and substance, to his very own inner life, and therefore wasn’t produced out of nothing.

Tertullian is clear that divine Word/Wisdom is uncreated by nature in that it didn’t come into being from nothing, but has always existed with and in God himself. Hence, when Tertullian states that God begot and created his own Wisdom/Word, he doesn’t mean that God brought it into existence from non-existence or non-being, but rather brought it forth out of himself, from his own uncreated Being. This is the very uncreated Wisdom/Word that Tertullian believes became Man and is now known as the Lord Jesus Christ.   

Another point which needs to be emphasized is that Tertullian identifies God’s Wisdom with the Spirit of God mentioned in 1 Corinthians 2:10-12, who alone knows the mind of the Lord. By this association, Tertullian wasn’t claiming that Jesus as Wisdom is the Holy Spirit since he expressly states throughout his writings that the Holy Spirit holds third place after Christ, which is why God is a Trinity of Persons/Beings within a unity of substance. Rather, Tertullian is emphasizing the fact of Christ possessing the very substance of God who himself is Spirit, which makes the prehuman Jesus a Spirit by nature in contrast to being a physical corporeal being. Christ only became physical by nature at his human conception.

The other point I want to make is in respect to Tertullian’s claim that God is older than the Son, and by virtue of this fact is nobler. Within the context of his response, Tertullian believes that Christ hasn’t always been the Son, but has always been God’s very own Reason/Wisdom/Word since God has never existed apart from and without his divine Reason. Christ became the Son when God begot him out of himself at the moment when he used him to produce creation out of nothing. This means that Tertullian thought that God is older than the Son, but not older than his uncreated Reason/Wisdom/Word, and it is only in the former sense that God can be viewed as being nobler than Christ by virtue of being older than him in terms of his Sonship and begetting. Yet as uncreated Reason/Wisdom/Word, Christ is just as old and, therefore, just as noble as God since they are identical in terms of substance, which is why Tertullian expressly states that the Unbegotten is not stronger or more powerful than the Begotten.
