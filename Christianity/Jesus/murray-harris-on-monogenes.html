Murray Harris on Monogenes

August 30, 2026
jesus god theology #Beautified

The following excerpt is taken from Murray J. Harris’ Jesus as God: The New Testament Use of Theos in Reference to Jesus, III. The Only Son, Who Is God (John 1:18), published by Baker Books, Grand Rapids, MI in 1992. All emphasis is mine.

50. Judg. 11:34; Tob. 3:15; 6:11 (A), 15 (א); 8:17; Ps. 21:21; 24:16; 34:17; Wisd. Sol. 7:22.

51. Josephus, Ant. 1:222, 5:264. Israel is called monogenes in Pss. Sol. 18:4; cf. 4 Ezra 6:68.

52. Luke 7:12; 8:42; 9:38. In Heb. 11:17 Isaac is designated the ho monogenes of Abraham, not as the only son he fathered (cf. Ishmael, Gen. 16:15) but as the only " son of promise" or his "beloved son.” Behind the use of monogenes in later Hellenistic Jewish writings and in the NT outside John, de Kruijf finds the theme of paradoxical divine intervention to save an only child who is in a critical situation, intervention that calls for the utmost trust and fidelity (113-17). (P. 85)

58. Interpreting monogenes in the light of their view of prototokos, the Arians took the mono- to indicate that only the Son was created directly by God, all other creatures being created through the Son (Pollard, Christology, 158, 169, 212-13).

59. Concerning King Monobazus of Adiabene, Josephus (Ant. 20:20) writes: "By Helena he had an older son than Izates named Monobazus, and other children by his other wives, but it was obvious that all his favor was focused on lzates, as if he were an only son (hos eis monogenes)."

60. Cf. Josephus, Ant. 1:222: " Now Isaac was very dearly loved by his father Abraham, since he was his only son (monogene onta) and born to him on the threshold of old age through God's bounty." For evidence that in the Orphic hymns monogenes is "a hyperbolic expression of affection" meaning "'darling' or as we say, Dear One," see J. R. Harris, "Athena" 65-69...

64. Yet it is inappropriate to dismiss, as some do, the possibility that monogenes means "only begotten" on the ground that monogennetos would then have been used, for this word is never found in extant Greek literature. (Pp. 85-86)

68. That monogenes does not relate to the virgin birth of Jesus is clear from the fact that Jesus' sonship was not inaugurated by the incarnation. First John 4:14 shows that the Son assumed the additional role of Savior of the world at the incarnation; the preexistent Son became the historical Savior. On the patristic concept of eternal generation and in particular on the role of Origen in the formulation of the concept, see Wiles, "Generation.”

69. LSJ 1144 s.v. Although appeal is often made to this lexicon in support of the equation monogenes = “unique," in fact it does not list John 1:14 (the only NT occurrence cited) under that meaning, but (with a "cf.") under "only, single." (P. 87)
