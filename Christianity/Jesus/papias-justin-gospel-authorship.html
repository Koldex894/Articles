Papias, Justin & Gospel Authorship

August 16, 2026
church-history gospel jesus christianity #Beautified

The early 4th century AD church historian Eusebius consulted the five-volume work on the sayings of the Lord Jesus, which was composed by Papias, the Bishop of Hierapolis. According to Papias’ own testimony he was a hearer/disciple of the Apostles and their followers. The 2nd century AD Christian apologist and holy martyr Irenaeus, who was the Bishop of Lyons, confirms that Papias was a disciple of the Apostle John and a companion of Polycarp, who himself was the Bishop of Smyrna and was also a martyr.

According to Eusebius, Papias wrote that Mark composed his Gospel around the preaching of Peter:

Chapter 39. The Writings of Papias.

The next witness to Gospel authorship is the 2nd century Christian apologist Justin Martyr. Justin quotes the material found in the four canonical Gospels and refers to them as the memoirs of the Apostles and their followers:

(1) It is remarkable that Eusebius castigates Papias’ intelligence and questions whether he directly learned from the Apostles and disciples of Christ, when Papias himself and Irenaeus both confirm that he did so. It seems the reason that Eusebius sought to undermine this claim is because Papias believed that the thousand-year reign of Christ was a literal physical event that would take place when the Lord Jesus returns to the earth. Papias even convinced many early writers and fathers to adopt this literal interpretation, including Irenaeus himself. Eusebius was of the view that Christ’s thousand-year reign should be interpreted allegorically, and had to find a way of getting around Papias’ witness. After all, Papias would have learned this interpretation from the Apostles such as John, and Eusebius knew that this posed a serious problem to his allegorical interpretation.
