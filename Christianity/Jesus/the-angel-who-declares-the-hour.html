An objection that is often used against the Deity of Christ is to cite the words of our Lord concerning the knowledge of the day and hour:

Since Jesus includes himself among those who do not know the hour of his coming in judgement against Jerusalem, this is taken as proof that he is not equal to God the Father.

One of the ways that Christians have explained these statements is to show that the Greek word for “knows” (eido) can mean to make known or to declare. This suggests that the words of our Lord should be rendered in the following manner:

In an attempt to refute this interpretation, certain Muslims and anti-Trinitarians provide the following counter example from the book of Revelation.

John the Apostle refers to an angel that was sent to declare that the hour of God’s judgment has come to pass. This shows that Matthew and Mark could not have possibly meant that no angel would make known the hour of God’s judgement, unless one wants to believe that the Holy Bible is contradicting itself.  

However, the supposed contradiction only exists in the imagination of the objector since this is nothing more than a caricature of the argument.

The Christian position in respect Matthew 24:36 and Mark 13:32 isn’t that men and angels can never make known the day or hour. Rather, the actual point is that it is solely the prerogative of the Father whether this datum will ever be made known. Once the Father has decided to reveal this bit of information then he will surely do so through the agency of his inspired emissaries, which includes angels and men.

In fact, this is precisely what the risen Lord Jesus expressly said to his disciples:

Notice the emphasis on the Father having the authority and sovereign freedom to determine the times and seasons for the restoration of the kingdom.
