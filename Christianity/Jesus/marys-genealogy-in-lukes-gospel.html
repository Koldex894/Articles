Mary’s Genealogy in Luke’s Gospel?

August 10, 2026
mary gospel jesus david #Beautified

Luke’s Gospel records a genealogy, which seems to be that of Mary’s even though Joseph is listed by name:

The reason why Luke would mention Joseph and not Mary is because Israelite genealogies are centered around men, due to being patriarchal in nature.

And when the man had no male heirs, only daughters, then his line would continue through his son-in-law, as we find in the case of the following Judahite:

An Egyptian slave sires a male heir for his father-in-law Sheshan in order to continue the lineage of his Judahite grandfather. Since Christian tradition going as far back as the first century teaches that Mary was the only child of her parents, it only makes sense that Joseph would carry out a similar role for his father-in-law that Sheshan’s Egyptian slave Jarha did for him.

Now when we add the fact that Luke 1-2 focus primarily on Mary, not Joseph, then this makes the case of the genealogy being Mary’s all the more likely.

In fact, a Latin commentary on the Gospels written by an African bishop of Aquileia, Italy in the fourth century (350) AD, states that there were already many Christian expositors who believed that Luke’s genealogy was that of the holy and blessed Virgin:

47 Matthew 1:17.

48 Cf. Matthew 1:12–16.

49 Cf. Deuteronomy 25:5–6.

50 Cf. Matthew 1:16; Luke 3:23.

51 John 15:15.

52 Matthew 1:1. In order to maintain the connections made by Fortunatianus, “generation” has been used for the Latin singular generatio in this biblical passage.

53 The latter half of this sentence could be translated in several ways, but line 164 confirms the interpretation adopted here.

54 Cf. Ephesians 5:23.

55 Cf. Genesis 49:3–12; 35:22; 34:1–31.

56 Cf. Genesis 49:9. (Fortunatianus of Aquileia, Commentary on the Gospels, English translation and introduction by H.A.G. Houghton in association with Lukas J. Dorfbauer [Walter de Gruyter GmbH, Berlin/Boston, 2017], pp. 6-7; emphasis mine)

Despite Fortunatianus’ rejection of this view he, nonetheless, acknowledges that there already were many believers who held this opinion from before the time he wrote his commentary in the mid 4th century AD.

St. Jerome seems to have believed this as well:

Jerome is clear that Joseph’s lineage includes Mary since she is also a descendant of David. Now what genealogy could this possibly be referring to if not that of Luke’s, seeing that Luke’s entire narrative is centered on Mary’s experiences? This is in contrast to Matthew’s Gospel where the focus centers on the perspective of Joseph.

Jerome is also aware of Julius Africanus’ view, which is adopted by both Eusebius and Fortunatianus, that Matthew 1 and Luke 3 are recording Joseph’s biological and legal genealogies. This is a result of Levirate marriages occurring within Joseph’s family tree, wherein a man sires an offspring from his deceased brother’s widow. The purpose of these unions was to prevent the deceased’s lineage being permanently cut off from the commonwealth of Israel (cf. Deut. 25:5-10).
