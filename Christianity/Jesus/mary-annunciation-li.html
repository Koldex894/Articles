After all, if the difference between the words yubashiroke and le ahaba lake imply that these are two separate events then he cannot posit the argument that the angels in Q. 3:45 refers to Gabriel's appearance to Mary in Q. 19:17. This, thereby, provides another clear-cut example of an error since the Quran makes a promise that fails to happen!

As it stands, this particular reference provides no support for the author's assertion that Mary may have been given general inspiration. In fact, Mary no more felt the inspiration as opposed to seeing and hearing the angels, then did Zechariah who, in the very same chapter, also encountered the angels and was commanded not to speak for three days because he doubted the possibility that he and his wife could be given a son in light of their situation (Q. 3:39-41).

We must conclude that none of the three explanations provided by the author sufficiently answer the problems and contradictions in the Quran's retelling of Jesus' birth.

Here, now, is a summation of the contradictions between these two versions as well as the data showing that the two Suras are narrating the same event:

  • The birth annunciation of John the Baptist appears in both accounts, right before the announcement of Jesus' birth (3:37-47; 19:2-15).
  • Q. 19:16 says that Mary went east in seclusion, with Q. 3:37 stating that she came under the care of Zechariah who would visit Mary in her sanctuary or chamber. This implies that both accounts place the birth annunciation during Mary's alleged stay at the temple in Jerusalem.
  • When told that she would conceive, Mary asks how this could be (Q. 3:47; Q. 19:20).
  • The reply given is that it is possible for Allah (Q. 3:47; Q. 19:21).
  • The version found in Q. 3 says that a group of angels appeared to Mary, whereas Q. 19 says it was God's Spirit who appeared to her in the form of a man.
  • In Q. 3:45 the angels tell Mary that Allah gives her glad tidings of a Word from him named the Messiah Jesus. Yet in Q. 19:19 God's Spirit tells her that he came to give her a faultless boy without naming the child.
