My aim in this article will be to show from the first chapter of Hebrews that the angels worship Jesus as YHWH, even though he is not the Father or the Holy Spirit.

The inspired writer begins by introducing God’s unique Son as being greater and more majestic than the angels for a variety of reasons.

Jesus is said to be greater than the angels by virtue of his being the Agent and Heir of all creation through whom the ages were created, being the One that personally sustains all things by his powerful command. This shows that it was the Son that created the heavenly host, and who is continually upholding their very existence.

The Son is also superior to the angels since he is exactly like God in every way, something which cannot be said of heavenly host, and is enthroned alongside God in heaven. In contrast to this, the author depicts the angels as ministers whom God created to serve.

It is for these reasons that God demands the whole entire angelic host to worship his Son.

With the foregoing in view, I now focus on the specific passage itself.

WHEN DO ANGELS WORSHIP JESUS?

To start with, biblical exegetes are divided over how to translate verse 6.

For instance, did the inspired author mean that the angels will worship the Son after God again brings him into the world? Or is the writer employing the term “again” to introduce another OT citation?

As the readers can see, the NIV understands Hebrews to be using the word palin (“again”) to group together a series of OT verses, which relate the point which the inspired author seeks to make.

However, other translators and scholars understand palin to be referring to Christ’s second coming, his return the world, which will be the time the angels will worship the Son.

The two specific interpretations offered by these varying translations have a direct affect in regards to the exact timing of the angelic worship of the Son.

If we opt for the view that palin is used to point to another OT verse then the meaning is that by bringing his Son in full view of the inhabited earth, God was making known to mankind that Jesus is the One whom the angels all worship.

In other words, the text is not specifying a time when the angels began to worship God’s unique Son, since they have always done so from the very moment the preincarnate Christ brought them into being. Rather, the point of the inspired writer is that the incarnation of Christ revealed to the inhabited world that there exists a unique divine Person who is intimately related to God as his beloved Son whom the angels have been worshiping from the beginning of their creation.     

If, however, we take the other interpretation then Hebrews is indicating that it is after the Son returns to the earth that the whole world will witness the entire angelic host worshiping Jesus as the Lord of all creation.
