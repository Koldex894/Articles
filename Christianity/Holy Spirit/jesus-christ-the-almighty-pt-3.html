I now arrive at the finale: Jesus Christ the Almighty! Pt. 2.

In this final part, I will provide further evidence that John intends to depict Jesus as the Almighty.

Recall that the Greek term for Almighty, i.e., Pantokrator, speaks to God’s sovereignty over all creation as the supreme King.

In light of this, the Evangelist sees Jesus appearing as a young male Lamb that had been slain but is alive, having seven horns and seven eyes which are God’s seven Spirits:

Keep in mind this fact that no created being in creation was worthy enough to approach God on his throne, since this will show that the Son and Spirit are not creatures.

The number seven in the Bible takes on the meaning of perfection, completion, that which lacks nothing. This is derived from the story of Genesis where God rested on the seventh day since everything in creation was complete and was very good:

That’s also why Revelation can say that it takes seven plagues to finish and complete God’s wrath:

Now according to Scripture, something that is complete is perfect since it lacks nothing:

Moreover, a horn according to Scripture is a reference to a king who possesses power, dominion and sovereignty:

With the foregoing in view, we are now ready to decode the symbolism and imagery that John saw.

The reason Jesus appears as a young male lamb is because he is the new Passover of the believers who was slain to redeem them from their bondage to sin:

The reason Christ has seven horns is because this points to his being the almighty, all-sovereign King, whose authority and power are complete and lack nothing. Jesus’ seven eyes point to his being omniscient, that he perfectly sees and is fully aware of all things that take place. These eyes being called God’s seven Spirits points to Christ’s union with the Holy Spirit, that the Son is present by the Spirit throughout the earth. This in turn points to the Holy Spirit being perfect and complete, since he lacks nothing also.

Elsewhere, John describes Jesus as not only possessing God’s seven Spirits, but also the seven stars that are the angels/bishops overseeing the seven churches:

Therefore, the symbolism of seven horns and seven eyes depicts Jesus as the Almighty King who oversees and superintends all creation. This is further corroborated from the fact of Jesus possessing seven eyes since these are the very eyes of YHWH himself:

That the risen Lamb possesses YHWH’s own eyes proves that he is YHWH God Almighty.

As if this weren’t remarkable enough, Christ and the Spirit are differentiated from every created being throughout all creation:

Since every creature in existence renders to Jesus the same exact worship and for the exact same duration that God receives, and since the Holy Spirit is joined to the Lamb as his seven eyes, this means that the Son and the Spirit are not part of creation. Rather, both Jesus and the Holy Spirit are on the same side that the Father is in the Creator/creation divide, proving that Father, Son and Spirit eternally exist as the one true and uncreated sovereign God of all creation.

In light of the above, could John have been any clearer in his witness to Jesus being the Almighty and to the Holy Spirit being essentially one with both the Father and the Son?

Unless indicated otherwise, scriptural references taken from the New International Version (NIV).
