Isaiah’s Priestly Servant

September 17, 2026
atonement isaiah #Beautified

Isaiah speaks of a righteous Servant who gives up his soul/life as a guilt offering so as to bear the sins of many:

Isaiah uses priestly language here since it was the function of the priests to bear away the sins of the people by making a sacrificial atonement in order to cleanse them and all the items, which had been made unclean because of their transgressions. If atonement wasn’t made for the individual then this meant that s/he would have to bear their own sins and die as a result.

The Day of Atonements

The priestly significance of the Suffering Servant can be clearly seen in this next example, which deals with the day on which the high priest entered the most holy place to make atonement for the transgressions of the entire nation and the tabernacle/temple, which had been profaned by the people’s sins:

Note that the priest lays his hands on the living goat in order to place upon it all the sins of Israel so it can then bear them away into the wilderness. This transference of sins only occurs after the other goat has been slaughtered and its blood presented on the altar, showing that sin can only be removed after the sacrificial death of the innocent victim takes place.

These examples conclusively prove that Isaiah has deliberately employed priestly language for the express purpose of describing the Servant as a priest who offers his own soul as the guiltless sacrificial victim, which God requires for atonement. The Servant does this so that he can then bear the transgressions of the people and make intercession for them, thereby procuring their forgiveness and redemption.
