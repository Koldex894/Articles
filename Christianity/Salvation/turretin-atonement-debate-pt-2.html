THE ELECT ARE BORN OBJECTS OF WRATH

Specific Calvinists erroneously interpret texts where Christ is said to have propitiated God to mean that God’s anger has been completely removed, and therefore is no longer angry at sinners (cf. Romans 3:25; Hebrews 2:17; 1 John 2:2; 4:10). They then extrapolate from this that Christ could not have died for all humans since many of them end up undergoing everlasting destruction. This means that their sins had not been propitiated since God would not have been angry with them if this were the case.  

The problem with this assertion is that it overlooks the fact that the Scriptures emphatically teach that Christ’s propitious sacrifice is only applied to those who place their trust in Jesus. And until and unless they do so, they remain in their sins and under God’s wrath:

Even the Calvinist has to admit that the elect, or those chosen for salvation from the foundation of the world, are not born justified and regenerated. Rather, they are born fallen sinners, being children of wrath and objects of divine displeasure, until the moment they are united to Christ through their faith:

What this shows is that not even the sins of the elect are propitiated without their first coming to saving faith in Christ. Therefore, they too are born under God’s wrath and displeasure as John Calvin himself stated in his exposition of 2 Corinthians 5:19:

CHRIST PRAYS FOR THE WORLD

Certain Calvinists argue that Christ’s atoning death is bound up with his role as intercessor, since an integral part of his saving work is his interceding for all those whom he died. They reason that Jesus only intercedes for those who come to God through him, which they assume proves that Christ did not die for all human beings (Cf. Romans 8:31-34; Hebrews 7:25).

There isn’t a single text of Scripture that states that Jesus ONLY intercedes for those who believe in or come to God through him. This is something that these particular Calvinists are reading into the Holy Bible.

In point of fact, the inspired Scriptures affirm the opposite point. The God-breathed writings actually depict Christ as praying and interceding for the salvation of the world which he distinguishes from all those who come to believe in him:
