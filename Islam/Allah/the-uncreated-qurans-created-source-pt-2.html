The Sunni Dilemma

These statements contradict the assertion that the Quran is Allah’s uncreated speech, which he supposedly revealed to Muhammad via Gabriel and by other means. Rather, the Quran actually came down from the preserved tablet, which Allah is supposed to have created, and in which he commanded his mystical talking pen to write it down along with everything that will take place in creation. Allah had the pen do this fifty-thousand-years before the creation of the heavens and the earth:  

This in turn raises additional problems and contradictions.

First, if Allah’s created talking pen wrote down the Quran along with everything else then it cannot be the uncreated speech of Allah, which emanated from him. Rather, it is something which came into being the moment the pen began to write it down, much like the rest of the words that would have been recorded within the tablet.

Now a Muslim may argue that the pen wrote down what Allah spoke to it of the Quran, and therefore the Quran isn’t created. The problem with this view is that this would be true of everything else that the pen recorded, namely, all of that would then be the words that Allah spoke. And if Allah speaking the Quran makes it uncreated then this would have to extend to every single word recorded by the pen. Yet the pen is supposed to have written down everything that creatures would say and do, which would then make their words, deeds, and even they themselves the uncreated speech of Allah.

Second, there isn’t a clear, unambiguous statement in the Quran where it refers to itself as the speech/word of Allah. Rather, there are verses that actually claim that the Quran is the word of a noble messenger!

Now unless Muslims want to claim that Allah is his own messenger, it should be clear that the Quran is not his word but the word of another.

Thus, both the Quran and Sunni traditions contradict the notion of the Muslim scripture being the uncreated speech of Muhammad’s god. It is rather words which the pen produced when it inscribed them in the heavenly tablet, which Allah created. The Quran even calls this tablet the mother of the book, meaning the source from which the Muslim book originates and came down to Muhammad.
