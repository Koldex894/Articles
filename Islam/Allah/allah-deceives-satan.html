Allah Deceives Satan

November 19, 2026
#Beautified allah quran satan

The Quran narrates a garbled version of a Jewish and Christian fable regarding the angelic host being commanded to worship Adam. In messing up the details, the narrator(s) end up depicting the Muslim deity as unjust and deceptive.

Here is the Muslim version of the fairytale, which is repeated throughout the Quran:

Notice that Satan is named Iblis and is said to be a jinn or genie. Notice also that the command was for the angels to worship Adam, which all of them did. Here is where the problem lies.

Jinn are not angels, but a different category of being from them. In fact, the Quran expressly states that angelic beings are not capable of disobeying Allah:

This explains why all the angels obeyed their lord in committing shirk, which is the sin of associating partners with Allah, by worshiping a creature. These beings do not have the free will to do otherwise.  

However, Iblis is not an angel and therefore was not included in this command to worship a creature. Why, then, did Allah condemn him for not obeying an injunction that did not include him?

To give an example of how unjust this is, suppose Allah commanded human beings to bow down towards the pagan shrine in Mecca, with all of them doing so except an angel who was among them. Would it be fair for Allah to punish the angel for not bowing down to the Kaaba, seeing that the order was given to human creatures, not angels?

To give a human analogy, suppose a gym instructor commanded all the men in the room to start doing push-ups, and all of them did so except a woman who happened to be among them. Would it be fair for the instructor to get angry with this woman for not hitting the floor to do push-ups, seeing that he specifically said men?

This may explain why the Quran records Iblis being angry at Allah for corrupting and deceiving him, and threatening to do the same to mankind:

Notice the travesty in all of this. 

The Islamic deity punishes Iblis for not obeying an order given only to the angels, not to the jinn, where they had to worship a creature, which itself is supposed to be a sin. All the angels did so except Iblis, who was a jinn, not an angel, and for that he is condemned for all eternity.

What Allah did to Iblis is truly wicked and reprehensible, and simply goes to show that Iblis was just another victim of Allah’s deceit and conniving.
