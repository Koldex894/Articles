Isaiah 14 in context

Here is the entire chapter of Isaiah 14 so that we can get an idea of the context and subject matter:

As the context itself shows the entire chapter is about God’s judgment upon the king of Babylon for all his wickedness, especially for arrogantly thinking that he could make himself like the Most High God. As such there is no explicit reference to Satan anywhere in the chapter.

However, it is quite possible that the passage is also referring to a spirit being who possessed or influenced the king of Babylon, one who desired to exalt himself over the rest of God’s spirit creatures, since the Bible makes it clear that human rulers are actually guided and controlled by spirits who rule over them:

The Holy Bible also refers to God punishing both the hosts of heaven and the earthly kings:

This verse supports the view that the spiritual hosts of heaven are directly influencing the decisions and actions of the rulers of the earth, which explains why God punishes them both.

In light of the above one can contend that Isaiah 14 refers to both the human king and the spirit being that controlled or influenced him, e.g. the inspired prophet is speaking of the destruction that God would bring upon both the king of Babylon and the spirit that actually ruled the people through him. However, since the chapter nowhere mentions Satan by name or some other wicked unclean spirit one cannot dogmatically assert that Isaiah 14:12 refers to the Devil. It is an inference that can be drawn on the basis of the overall testimony of Holy Scripture, but it is not something explicitly stated within the text itself. Besides, it may have been some other wicked spirit that influenced the Babylonian ruler instead of Satan.

How many morning stars are there?

In order for this Muslim neophyte’s conclusion that Jesus is the same as the morning star mentioned in Isaiah 14:12 to be logically valid he must first demonstrate that the Bible teaches that there is only one such figure. He must show that the morning star is an exclusive title used for a specific entity which is non-transferable. At the very least he must demonstrate that the New Testament writers were using this phrase in the same sense that Isaiah used it since this is the only way his objection to the connection between Jesus’ I AM statements and Exodus 3:14 will carry any weight. As we have already documented elsewhere (*; *) Jesus uses the I AM in the same sense that Yahweh does all throughout the OT, specifically the books of Deuteronomy and Isaiah.

The dawagandist must also prove that both Isaiah 14:12 and Revelation 22:16 use the same words, otherwise he fails to make his case.

However, if there happens to be more than one morning star or if the passages in question use different words then the neophyte’s entire case collapses and his objection against the Deity of Christ is shown to be without merit.
