The Testimony of the Christian Scriptures

The New Testament agrees with the Hebrew Scriptures in that it proclaims that Jesus the Messiah is an eternal King who rules over the entire creation forever, and that every creature must serve and worship him. It even quotes the same OT references that the rabbinic sources do to substantiate this point. All scriptural quotations taken from the Authorized King James Version (AV):

The Lord Jesus himself entered into Jerusalem riding on a colt, the foal of a donkey, in deliberate fulfillment of Zechariah 9:9-10, a prophecy that we had cited in the first part of our discussion: Zechariah 9:9-10

In doing so, Jesus was making an explicit statement to the inhabitants of Jerusalem that he is the divine King whom the prophet Zechariah announced was coming to save his people.

The Lord Jesus also quoted Psalm 110:1 to affirm that the Messiah is more than David’s Son, since he is also his sovereign Lord who reigns from God’s right hand: Psalm 110:1

Thus, the NT presents Jesus as the eternal King who reigns from the right hand side of God, i.e. from God’s own throne, over the entire creation. It also depicts Christ as embodying the very essence and presence of God, and further portrays him as receiving worship from every creature in recognition of his exalted status as God’s unique Son, Savior and Lord. In this regard, the inspired Christian Scriptures are perfectly compatible with both the Hebrew Bible and the rabbinic sources, even going as far as to cite the same OT texts that the rabbis did regarding the Person and work of the Messiah!

With the foregoing in perspective it is now time for us to see how well Muhammad fares in light of the teaching of the Jewish Bible and rabbinic tradition concerning the Messiah.
