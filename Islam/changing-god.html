This leads us to our next point. God repenting from destroying the entire people doesnt necessarily imply that God changed his mind. If anything, it proves that God is immutable and remains consistent with his nature. Let us explain what we mean.

Suppose that God continues to bless indefinitely a nation that has turned away from righteousness, would he be holy and just? Of course not since an infinitely holy God cannot simply overlook persistent sin and rebellion but must address and punish all evildoers. Likewise, what if God destroyed a nation that earnestly sought forgiveness and repented of their wickedness? Would God be merciful and kind? We all know what the answer is.

Here is the point. Since God cannot deny himself, but must always remain faithful, he will either pull back from punishing a nation that has repented or bring punishment upon a people that has turned away from his righteousness. Summary Analysis

Here is what we have seen thus far:

A careful reading of Exodus 32 and 33, as well as an examination of the Bible as a whole, shows that Gods threats of destruction and disaster are not the last word, they are not irrevocable.

Gods warnings are often conditional (which may not appear in that particular text but which is implied nonetheless since it is mentioned elsewhere - cf. Jeremiah 18:5-12; Ezekiel 18:20-23, 30-32 etc.), with the condition being that he will only carry out his threat of destruction if the people do not repent.

The reason Gods warnings often carry a condition is due to his very nature, due to his being a loving and merciful God who waits patiently for his servants to repent since he doesnt delight in destroying the wicked but saving them.

In fact, God often uses threats to lead people to repentance, to move them to turn from their wickedness and live.

Yet God cannot simply forgive the injustices and crimes of the people without this compromising his holiness and justice. Therefore, Gods righteousness must be met and his wrath must be appeased before he can forgive the wicked.

God has provided vicarious sacrifice, substitutionary atonement, to punish the crimes of the wicked so that his justice and holiness can be satisfied and therefore perfectly maintained.

Yet these sacrifices could only provide temporary satisfaction due to their limited and finite value. This explains why they had to be offered over and over again. Ultimately these offerings prefigured and foreshadowed the only sacrifice that could provide eternal satisfaction to God, the only offering whose value is infinite. They pointed to the vicarious death of the Lord Jesus, the Spotless Lamb of God.

In fact, the mediation and intercession of Moses, Aaron and the others also foreshadowed the Person and work of the Lord Jesus. They were all pointing to the mediation and intercession of Christs eternal High Priesthood:

The foregoing shows that God wasnt necessarily changing his mind when he relented from bringing disaster, since his intention isnt to destroy but to lead sinners to repentance and forgiveness.

Another reason God gives out warnings is to show that his destruction of these people is well deserved and just since, instead of doing what is right by turning from their wicked ways, they persist in their rebellion and wickedness.

Putting it another way, since God knows everything perfectly he already knows whether these individuals will repent or not. Yet he warns them anyway in order to demonstrate his great endurance and tolerance since he doesnt rush in to destroy the wicked but waits patiently before bringing complete annihilation. These warnings also demonstrate why such persons fully deserve the punishment they receive since, no matter how many threats are given or disciplinary measures are taken, no matter how long God waits patiently for them to turn, they continue to act wickedly and corruptly.
