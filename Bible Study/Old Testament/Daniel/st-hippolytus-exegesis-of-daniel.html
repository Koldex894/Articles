St. Hippolytus’ Exegesis of Daniel

June 11, 2025
daniel prophecy messiah jesus-christ church-history saints #Beautified 2025

In this post I will be sharing St. Hippolytus’ lengthy explanation of the Daniel’s prophecies as it relates to the coming of Christ. It is taken from the work titled, Some Exegetical Fragments of Hippolytus.

Before I do so I provide a summary of this blessed saint’s awe-inspiring exegesis so the readers will know what to look for.

  • Christ appeared several times throughout the book of Daniel. For instance, it was Christ whom the prophet saw in 10:4-6, and it was Christ who was hovering over the river in 12:5-7. Christ was also the One who appeared as the fourth Man in the fire to save Daniel’s three friends.
  • Christ is the Son of Man and the Ancient of Days mentioned throughout 7:9-27.
  • The angel whom God sent to accompany Moses was Michael. This means that St. Hippolytus took the view that Jesus is the God who spoke to Moses and sent his angel Michael to assist Israel during the Exodus.
  • Daniel 9:20-27 foretells of two Christs that would arise, the first being Joshua the son of Jehozadak, and the other being our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. Hippolytus further states that Jesus is the Most Holy whom Gabriel announced would be anointed.
  • There are two abominations spoken of by Daniel. The first is Antiochus Epiphanes IV, and the other is the Antichrist who will arise in the latter days when Elijah and Enoch appear as the two witnesses of Revelation 11, whom he will then murder. Hippolytus goes on to provide a thorough exegesis of the history of Antiochus’ campaigns, and that of Alexander, to show how these events were perfectly foretold by the holy prophet in Daniel 8-11.
  • St. Hippolytus believes that six thousand years must pass and then Christ will descend on earth to reign with his saints for a thousand years. He bases this on the creation account where God created the heavens and earth in six days but then rested on the seventh day, making it the Sabbath. This means that this blessed expositor took the thousand-year reign mentioned in Rev. 20:1-10 as having a literal fulfillment. He didn’t interpret the thousand year reign symbolically or allegorically.
  • Mary is the pure Ark of gold from whom the Savior took his holy flesh.
  • Susannah’s story serves as a type and foreshadow of the Church as the Bride of Christ being slanderously attacked by her enemies who hate the risen Lord.

With the foregoing in perspective, I now quote this holy servant’s rather lengthy exposition, only omitting certain sections that seemed to me to be superfluous. All emphasis will be mine.
