In this section we are going to take a look at what the Christian Scriptures have to say concerning Jesus’ work of salvation and justification.

Christ – the God who Justifies and Saves

The NT writings describe Christ in the words of Isaiah 49 and 53, both of which speak of Yahweh’s Servant being sent to atone and intercede for sinners in order that he might bring God’s salvation to the nation of Israel and to the Gentiles:

Moreover, the inspired Scriptures repeatedly refer to him as the Savior whom God sent to save the world:

That’s not all they say, since the God-breathed revelations clearly attest that Jesus came to save people from their sins in order to justify them, or to declare and make them righteous before God:

These sacred writings attest that the way Christ did this is by taking on the punishment of our sins by dying in our place, so as to offer his life as a vicarious sacrifice. In fact, this is the unanimous testimony of the NT writings:

This next passage highlights just how remarkable a Savior Jesus truly is:

Christ has purchased an innumerable host of individuals from every nation by his precious blood, making them worthy to stand before God’s presence forever and ever!

And on the basis of his vicarious death the risen Lord is now interceding in the very heavenly presence of his Father for every true believer, thereby procuring and guaranteeing their eternal redemption:

Now it only makes perfect sense that the Christian Scriptures would teach as a whole the necessity and centrality of Christ’s vicarious sacrifice for salvation and justification. After all, this is the message that Jesus commissioned and sent his blessed disciples to proclaim throughout the entire world:

With the foregoing in perspective, the only way that Jesus could ever be able to save and justify so many lives is if he himself is truly God in nature since, as we saw in the previous parts the inspired Scriptures emphatically insist that this is a divine work which only Yahweh can accomplish.

Lo and behold, this is precisely what the NT proclaims about Jesus, namely, that he himself is Yahweh God in the flesh that has come to shed his blood in order save and purify a people for himself so as to make them part of his church, which is his spiritual body.

Amen! Come Lord Jesus, come! We profess and believe that you are indeed the divine Messiah, the very God-Man whom the prophets announced would come into the world to save and justify sinners, as well as to judge the world in perfect righteousness. Amen!
